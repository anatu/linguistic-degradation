# PLAN.md — Distribution Stagnation PoC

## Research Question

Does training on progressively synthetic-contaminated data destroy a model's ability to comprehend genuinely novel linguistic constructions — not just degrade perplexity, but eliminate the representational capacity for out-of-distribution language?

## Core Hypothesis

When models are trained on data increasingly dominated by previous-generation LLM outputs, they lose the ability to perform zero-shot comprehension of held-out linguistic innovations. This is not a scaling problem or a data-quantity problem — it is a structural competence ceiling imposed by recursive interpolation within a narrowing distribution.

---

## Prior Work: Shumailov et al. and Model Collapse

### The Foundational Result

This experiment builds on and extends the model collapse framework introduced by Shumailov et al. in "The Curse of Recursion: Training on Generated Data Makes Models Forget" (arXiv:2305.17493, 2023; published in Nature, vol. 631, pp. 755–759, 2024).

Their core finding: when generative models are trained recursively on their own outputs, the tails of the original data distribution disappear irreversibly. They demonstrated this across three model families — Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs), and LLMs — establishing that model collapse is a general phenomenon affecting all learned generative models, not an architecture-specific bug. The LLM experiments used OPT-125M fine-tuned recursively on its own generations, showing progressive degradation from coherent English text to repetitive, nonsensical output (the famous "jackrabbit" example where an input about architecture devolved into text about different-colored rabbit tails).

### Their Methodology (and Where We Diverge)

Shumailov et al.'s experimental design followed a **full replacement** protocol: at each generation, the entire training set was replaced with synthetic data from the previous model. This is the maximally pessimistic scenario — it demonstrates collapse is possible, but doesn't model the realistic case where synthetic data accumulates alongside human data.

Key methodological features of their work:
- **Generational loop:** Model M(n) is trained on data generated by M(n-1), with M(0) trained on real data.
- **Full replacement:** Each generation trains on 100% synthetic data from the prior generation.
- **Metrics:** Perplexity, output diversity, and qualitative inspection of generated text.
- **Scale:** OPT-125M (125M parameters) for the LLM experiments; smaller models for VAE/GMM.

Subsequent work has extended this in important ways:
- Gerstgrasser et al. ("Is Model Collapse Inevitable?", arXiv:2404.01413, 2024) showed that if real and synthetic data **accumulate** (rather than synthetic replacing real), collapse can be avoided. This is important context — it means the data mixing ratios in our experiment are a critical design choice.
- Dohmatob et al. ("How Bad is Training on Synthetic Data?", arXiv:2404.05090, 2024) provided statistical formalization showing that collapse is unavoidable with 100% synthetic training but can be bounded below a threshold when mixing real and synthetic data.

### What This Experiment Adds

Our experiment asks a **qualitatively different question** than Shumailov et al. Their work measures **distributional degradation** — does the model's output distribution narrow and lose fidelity to the original? Our work measures **competence loss on genuinely novel inputs** — does the model lose the ability to *comprehend* linguistic phenomena it has never seen?

The distinction matters because:
1. **Shumailov's metrics (perplexity, output diversity) can miss our phenomenon.** A model could maintain reasonable perplexity on a contaminated distribution while completely losing the representational machinery to process out-of-distribution constructions. Our "linguistic novelty benchmark" is designed to catch exactly this.
2. **We use mixed contamination ratios, not full replacement.** Following the insight from Gerstgrasser et al., we sweep contamination ratios from 0% to 90% to find the threshold at which competence degrades — and separately run a compounding arm to model the realistic accumulation scenario.
3. **We measure comprehension, not just generation.** Shumailov et al. evaluated the quality of model *outputs*. We evaluate the model's ability to *understand inputs* it has never been trained on. This is the "competence ceiling" claim — that synthetic contamination doesn't just make outputs worse, it makes the model fundamentally unable to reason about novel language.

In short: Shumailov et al. showed that recursive synthetic training causes the distribution to collapse. We're testing whether it also causes the model's *capacity for linguistic extrapolation* to collapse — a stronger and more consequential claim.

## Experiment Overview

**Design:** Multi-generational training pipeline where each generation's training data is progressively contaminated with synthetic text from the prior generation's model. Evaluation measures comprehension of held-out linguistic phenomena that are structurally absent from the synthetic-contaminated distribution.

**Scale:** Minimal. Character-level or small-BPE transformer on a single GPU. The goal is directional signal, not SOTA.

**Key independent variable:** Synthetic contamination ratio across generations.

**Key dependent variable:** Zero-shot comprehension accuracy on a held-out "linguistic novelty" benchmark.

## Phase 1: Data Preparation

### 1.1 Base Corpus
- Use a small, diverse English text corpus as the "ground truth" human distribution.
- Recommended: ~50MB of mixed-register English text. Options:
  - WikiText-103 (encyclopedia register)
  - OpenWebText subset (web register)
  - A mix of both for register diversity
- This corpus represents "human language circa era 0."

### 1.2 Held-Out Linguistic Novelty Benchmark
- **This is the most important part of the experiment.** Hand-curate a small evaluation set (~200-500 examples) of linguistic phenomena that require genuine comprehension of novel constructions.
- Categories to include:
  1. **Novel slang/vernacular** — e.g., constructions from AAVE, Gen-Z slang, internet-native syntax ("no cap," "it's giving," stressed BIN constructions like "I been knew that")
  2. **Code-switching** — sentences mixing registers or languages mid-stream
  3. **Creative neologisms** — novel compound words, back-formations, semantic shifts (e.g., "to gaslight," "doom-scrolling" — things that didn't exist before a certain era)
  4. **Rhetorical subversion** — irony, sarcasm, deliberate rule-breaking for effect ("not me doing X," "say less")
  5. **Pragmatic inference** — utterances whose meaning depends on social context, not literal parse (e.g., "that's... a choice")
- Format: Each example is a (context, question, correct_answer) triple testing whether the model can infer the meaning/function of the novel construction from context alone.
- **Critical constraint:** None of these constructions should appear in the base training corpus. They represent "linguistic innovations the model has never seen."

### 1.3 Contamination-Free Validation Set
- Standard held-out split from the base corpus for tracking perplexity (the boring metric).
- Kept separate from the novelty benchmark.

## Phase 2: Generational Training Loop

### 2.1 Architecture
- Small GPT-2-style transformer. Suggested starting point:
  - 6 layers, 6 heads, 384 embedding dim
  - Context window: 256 tokens
  - ~10M parameters
- Use a fixed architecture across all generations. Architecture is not a variable.

### 2.2 Generation 0 (Baseline)
1. Train model M0 on the pure human base corpus until convergence (or fixed step budget).
2. Evaluate M0 on:
   - Validation perplexity (standard metric)
   - Linguistic novelty benchmark (the metric we care about)
3. Save M0 checkpoint.

### 2.3 Generation N (N = 1, 2, 3, ...)
1. Use model M(N-1) to generate synthetic text:
   - Sample from M(N-1) using nucleus sampling (top-p = 0.95, temperature = 1.0).
   - Generate enough synthetic text to create the desired contamination ratio.
2. Construct training corpus for generation N:
   - Mix human base corpus + synthetic text at a specified ratio.
   - Contamination ratios to sweep: **[0%, 10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 90%]**
   - Each ratio is a separate experimental arm — NOT sequential (i.e., the 50% arm uses 50% synthetic from M0 or the prior generation, not compounded).
3. Train model MN from scratch on the mixed corpus (same architecture, same step budget).
4. Evaluate MN on both metrics.
5. Repeat for K generations (suggest K=5 for PoC).

### 2.4 Compounding Variant (Secondary)
- In addition to the above, run one arm where contamination compounds:
  - Gen 1: 25% synthetic from M0
  - Gen 2: 25% synthetic from M1 (which was itself trained on contaminated data)
  - Gen 3: 25% synthetic from M2
  - ... and so on for K=5 generations
- This simulates the realistic "internet accumulation" scenario.

## Phase 3: Evaluation

### 3.1 Metrics

| Metric | What It Measures | How |
|--------|-----------------|-----|
| val_perplexity | Standard distribution fit | Cross-entropy on held-out base corpus |
| novelty_accuracy | Linguistic comprehension | Accuracy on the novelty benchmark (multiple choice or cloze) |
| novelty_log_prob | Confidence on novel constructions | Mean log-probability assigned to correct completions in novelty benchmark |
| generation_diversity | Output distribution breadth | Distinct-N (1,2,3-gram) on a fixed set of prompted generations |
| semantic_coherence | Ability to use novel constructions | BLEU/ROUGE or manual scoring on "use this construction in a sentence" tasks |

### 3.2 Key Comparisons
- **Primary:** Plot novelty_accuracy as a function of contamination ratio, faceted by generation number. The hypothesis predicts a monotonic decline that steepens with each generation.
- **Secondary:** Compare the compounding arm to the fixed-ratio arms. Compounding should show faster degradation at equivalent nominal ratios.
- **Control:** val_perplexity may stay flat or even improve (since synthetic data is "clean" for the base distribution). If novelty_accuracy drops while perplexity is stable, that's the strongest evidence for your thesis — the model is getting "better" at the impoverished distribution while losing competence on genuine novelty.

### 3.3 Visualization
- Heatmap: X = contamination ratio, Y = generation number, color = novelty_accuracy
- Line plot: novelty_accuracy over generations for the compounding arm
- Scatter: val_perplexity vs. novelty_accuracy across all experimental conditions (looking for divergence)

### 4.2 Implementation Order
1. **Start with `config.py`** — define all hyperparameters, ratios, generation count.
2. **`model/gpt.py`** — implement the small GPT. Keep it dead simple. Use PyTorch.
3. **`data/prepare_base.py`** — download WikiText-103 or equivalent, tokenize, save.
4. **`benchmark/novelty_examples.jsonl`** — create 50 examples minimum for the PoC (can be synthetic/templated for now — see note below).
5. **`train.py`** — standard training loop with logging.
6. **`data/generate_synthetic.py`** — load checkpoint, generate N tokens, save.
7. **`data/mix_data.py`** — combine base + synthetic at specified ratio.
8. **`eval/`** — implement all evaluation metrics.
9. **`run_experiment.py`** — wire everything together.
10. **`analyze.py`** — visualization.

### 4.3 Critical Note on the Novelty Benchmark
For the PoC, it is acceptable to bootstrap the novelty benchmark using a combination of:
- Manually written examples (highest quality, do as many as feasible)
- LLM-assisted generation (use Claude to generate examples, then manually verify/edit)
- Templated examples (e.g., fill-in-the-blank with novel slang from a curated list)

The benchmark should be formatted as multiple-choice cloze tasks:
```json
{
  "context": "When she saw the price tag, she said 'that's a whole ___'",
  "options": ["mood", "situation", "vibe", "problem"],
  "correct": "mood",
  "category": "novel_slang",
  "construction": "that's a whole [noun] — emphatic reaction expression",
  "era": "2018+"
}
```

The key constraint is that the constructions tested must be **absent from the base training corpus**. This is what makes the evaluation meaningful — we're testing whether the model can reason about things it has never seen, not whether it memorized them.

### 4.4 Computational Budget
- Each training run: ~10-30 minutes on a single consumer GPU (or CPU with reduced steps)
- Full experiment (6 ratios × 5 generations + 1 compounding arm): ~6-10 hours
- Should be runnable overnight on a MacBook with MPS or a single cloud GPU

### 4.5 Reproducibility
- Fix all random seeds
- Log all hyperparameters to a JSON file per run
- Save checkpoints for every generation
- Use deterministic PyTorch settings where possible

## Success Criteria for the PoC

This PoC is successful if it produces **directional signal** on any of the following:

1. **Novelty accuracy declines with contamination ratio** while perplexity stays flat or improves — this is the strongest possible signal for the distribution stagnation thesis.
2. **Compounding generations show steeper decline** than fixed-ratio arms — evidence for the "recursion trap" where each generation's interpolation narrows the next generation's representational capacity.
3. **Generation diversity (Distinct-N) drops** monotonically with contamination — the model's output distribution is collapsing even if it "looks" fine by perplexity.

Even negative results are informative. If the model maintains novelty comprehension under heavy contamination, that constrains the thesis and suggests the problem (if it exists) may only emerge at larger scales or with more subtle evaluation.

---

## What This PoC Does NOT Attempt

- Full-scale replication of model collapse results (already published)
- Training at frontier scale
- Human evaluation of generated text quality
- Modeling the actual dynamics of internet text accumulation over time
- Formal information-theoretic analysis of distribution narrowing

These are all important for a full paper but out of scope for directional signal.
